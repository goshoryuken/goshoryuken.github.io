Career goals

I want to work on the RTL-to-GDSII flow and FPGA architectures, building on my experience with SiliconJackets and Sharc Lab.

Own the digital implementation flow

My long-term goal is to take digital hardware from RTL through verification, synthesis, and physical implementation to GDSII. I’m interested in how the stages fit together: how an RTL decision affects synthesis, how floorplanning and placement affect routing, and how the resulting implementation meets timing, area, and power constraints.

My work with SiliconJackets has given me experience with an RV32I-subset CPU, a Sky130 flow, SRAM macro placement, timing analysis, clock gating, and DRC/LVS debugging. I want to develop the depth to own a block through that flow, explain the tradeoffs behind it, and diagnose problems across tool boundaries.

Build stronger verification habits

My current experience includes directed tests, constrained-random stimulus, a reference model, scoreboards, assertions, and coverage analysis. I want to strengthen those methods and learn more about UVM and formal verification. Each design requirement should have a check that can detect a violation, and each coverage gap should have an explanation.

A practical next step is a reproducible regression that checks the behavior of the RTL and its implementation. I want clear reporting of failures, constraints, and remaining gaps so that a clean run means something specific.

Go deeper from RTL to GDSII

I want more practice with timing constraints, floorplanning, clock-tree synthesis, placement, routing, and timing closure. My existing work includes tracing a path with roughly thirty-three cells of logic and finding tie-off problems during LVS after a clock-gating change. Those experiences have made me interested in understanding the reports well enough to identify the cause of a failure before changing the design.

I plan to compare implementation changes using timing, area, power, and physical verification results. My goal is to build a repeatable process: establish a baseline, change one part of the RTL or flow, rerun the relevant checks, and document what improved and what became worse.

Continue FPGA research

In Sharc Lab, I work on 3D FPGA architectures and the VTR and OpenFPGA toolchains. I want to continue exploring how architecture changes propagate through parsing, packing, placement, timing, and RTL generation. My interest is in programmable digital hardware and the tools that make new architectures usable.

The upcoming full-time ECE internship at GTRI, starting spring 2027, is another opportunity to explore FPGAs in the context of RF systems. I expect to work with radio-hardware control, integration, and testing, and I want to connect that experience with my research and digital implementation work.

Measure progress through working designs

My near-term priorities are a stronger verification flow, more experience closing timing and physical checks, and well-documented FPGA research results. I also want to improve the scripts and documentation that make each flow reproducible. A finished design, a reliable regression, and a clear explanation of the implementation tradeoffs are the outcomes I want to build toward.
