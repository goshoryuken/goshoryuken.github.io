Career goals

From here to there

Long term, I want to design the chips, and the tools, that make new kinds of hardware practical. Here's the plan, built from my ECE roadmap. It will change, and that's the point of writing it down.

Where I want to end up

Ten years from now I want to be a digital design or hardware architecture engineer working on custom silicon: CPUs, accelerators, FPGAs, or the 3D-stacked chips I'm researching now. I'm drawn to the roles between architecture and implementation, where you decide what the hardware should be and have to prove it can be built. My projects so far have covered both ends, from a CPU on an FPGA to a RISC-V core taken all the way to layout.

Whether that path runs through industry right after my B.S. or through graduate school first is an open question I want to answer with evidence: by doing research seriously, and by doing at least one internship in industry before I decide.

The roadmap

  1. Year 1 · 2025–26

    Foundation, and my first CPU

    Core math and programming, Digital System Design, and a pipelined CPU running on an FPGA. Then a CUDA logic simulator over the summer.

  2. Year 2 · 2026–27 · now

    Research and real design flows

    Move from building on my own to working inside real tools and teams.

    • Keep pushing the 3D FPGA work in the Sharc Lab; aim to present it at an undergraduate research symposium
    • Finish the SiliconJackets RTL-to-GDS flow and take on a larger block next semester
    • Land a summer 2027 internship in digital design, verification or physical design
  3. Year 3 · 2027–28

    Go deeper in architecture and VLSI

    Take the electives that match the work I want to do, and test it in a real job.

    • Computer architecture and VLSI electives (course numbers)
    • Add caches and a UVM testbench to my CPU, as planned in its roadmap
    • Aim for a co-authored paper or poster from the lab
    • Second internship or co-op, ideally at a semiconductor company
  4. Year 4 · 2028–29

    Decide: industry or graduate school

    Use three years of research and at least one internship to make that call on evidence.

    • Senior design project in digital hardware
    • Apply to M.S./Ph.D. programs or full-time roles, whichever the evidence points to
  5. After · 2029+

    Design hardware that didn't exist before

    Work on the architecture and implementation of custom silicon, and keep one foot in the tools that make it possible.

Gaps I'm closing

Writing this plan made a few gaps obvious. My verification so far has been hand-built class-based testbenches; industry runs on UVM, so that's next. Most of my work is digital, but 3D integration is ultimately a physical problem (wires, capacitance and heat), so I want stronger analog and circuits fundamentals. And I want more practice explaining technical work to people outside my field, which is part of why this site exists. Edit this to match the gaps you found in your own ECE Roadmap.
