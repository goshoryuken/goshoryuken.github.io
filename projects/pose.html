pose. — a wearable that notices when you slouch

A four-person hackathon build: a small ESP32 wearable that detects slouching on-device and syncs to a web dashboard. It won Best Use of TigerData at HackGT 13. I owned the hardware and firmware.

The problem

Long study sessions make it easy to stop noticing how you sit. We wanted something that would give students measurable feedback about their posture and small, encouraging goals, without being a medical device.

My part: the device

The wearable is an ESP32 with a BNO055 orientation sensor (IMU), read over I²C. All posture classification happens on the device; only the results go over Bluetooth Low Energy to the browser, so the web app never has to interpret raw sensor data.

  • Calibration. Press a button while sitting upright, and that becomes your personal baseline. Everything after is measured relative to it.
  • Angle wraparound. Orientation angles jump from +180° to −180°. Without handling that, turning slightly could look like a huge lean.
  • Persistence window. Reaching for a coffee isn't slouching. A lean only counts after 10 continuous seconds, and you have to be upright for 3 seconds before it resets.
  • Timing that doesn't break. Elapsed times use unsigned subtraction, so they stay correct even when the ESP32's millisecond counter wraps around.

The rest of the system

My teammates built the Ruby on Rails app: accounts, friend groups with weekly rankings, and a dashboard. Readings are stored in a TimescaleDB hypertable on Tiger Cloud, which is what the TigerData prize was for. My job was to make the boundary between them clean: the device publishes cumulative posture snapshots over BLE, and the browser forwards them to the server.

What I learned

Working on a team under a hackathon deadline means agreeing on interfaces first. Once the BLE message format was settled, the hardware and the web app could be built in parallel and meet in the middle. Add a photo of the wearable if you have one, and a line about the weekend in your own words.
